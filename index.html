RESEARCH DESIGN

Anatomy of a Flop

Research question

Within established creators (100k+ subscribers), holding channel, production quality, budget, authority, and niche roughly constant, which metadata and topic-demand features distinguish videos that flop (≤0.2× the channel's prior-90-day median views) from videos that go viral (≥3× that median)?

Design

Each video is compared to its own channel's rolling median of the prior 90 days — not a global average. This within-channel natural experiment removes cross-channel confounds.

  • Outcome: log10(views / prior_90d_median_views); binary flop/viral flags as secondary.
  • Inclusion: English videos ≥ 90 days old at collection; ≥ 5 prior uploads in the window.
  • Sampling: 4 niches × 3 subscriber bands, ~40 channels, pre-registered stratified random.

Features

  • Baseline: title length, has number, has question, caps ratio, tag count, duration, publish hour/day, time since last upload.
  • Topic: primary title entity (Plan 2).
  • Topic demand: Wikipedia pageviews at publish time (Plan 2).

Analysis phases

  1. Descriptive — flop vs. viral on every feature (FDR-corrected, channel-level mixed effects).
  2. Predictive — logistic baseline vs. gradient boosting, per-channel cross-validation, SHAP.
  3. Matched pairs — within-channel flop↔viral pairs matched on topic (sensitivity).

Deliverables

An open metadata-only dataset (data/videos.csv + codebook) and a publishable paper.
